About

About the movement

Young people should not have to leave their communities to change them.

ZLYM is an emerging youth-led movement. It helps young people who notice something missing in their school, town or neighbourhood move from an idea to a realistic project, even when they lack a mentor, a team, resources, or a clear place to begin.

It is not a page of scholarships, competitions and certificates. Its central purpose is to help young people create opportunities and initiatives in their own communities. Information may be shared when useful, but announcements are not the movement's main work.

Every meaningful change can begin at zero. What matters is what we choose to build from that point.

Principles

What we hold to.

Local ownership
Projects respond to real needs identified by the people who experience them. Young people shape and lead initiatives in their own communities.
Shared learning
Members exchange knowledge, approaches and honest lessons across communities and borders. Learning does not stay with one person or one project.
Real contribution
Participation means meaningful effort and tangible work, not titles, attendance, certificates or the appearance of activity.
Collaboration without uniformity
International collaboration never means imposing one solution everywhere. Ideas are understood, adapted and applied according to local realities.

How it works

Four stages, one line.

01

Notice

Identify a real need, challenge, gap or overlooked possibility in your local community: a school, a neighbourhood, a town. The starting point is understanding what is actually needed.

02

Shape

Turn the observation into a clear, realistic project: its purpose, the people it aims to help, the resources needed and the steps required. A good project does not have to be enormous.

03

Build

Take practical steps, alone or with a team. The movement aims to provide guidance, shared learning, tools and support where possible, while members keep real ownership of the work.

04

Pass it on

Share what worked, what did not, what the team learned and what another community could adapt. The goal is to spread the ability to create impact, not only finished projects.

Membership and applications

Two ways to take part.

General Member

For young people who want to join, contribute their skills, learn with others and take part in local and collaborative work: identify needs, develop project ideas, contribute to existing projects and exchange experience with members in other communities and countries.

No finished idea and no previous experience required. Curiosity, commitment, willingness to learn and readiness to contribute are enough to start.

Apply as a General Member

Country Ambassador

Ambassadors help introduce ZLYM in their country: presenting the movement, connecting committed people, helping build a local network, supporting local projects, and linking local experience to the wider international network. In the initial stage, the intended structure is one ambassador per country.

Applying is an expression of interest and does not automatically mean appointment. Every application is reviewed.

Apply as a Country Ambassador

Age and safeguarding. The movement is for young people aged 12 and above. Participation, especially any in-person activity, must follow appropriate safeguarding practices, with guardian consent where applicable.

Global collaboration

Local roots. Global connections.

A project may begin in one school or one small town, but what it teaches does not have to stay there. Young people living in different realities can exchange methods, compare local challenges, share what worked and what failed, and adapt useful ideas to their own communities.

The aim is not for every community to follow one model. Different places have different needs, cultures and resources, so collaboration keeps ownership local.

The international network is a developing vision and structure. No country chapters or cross-border projects exist yet.

Founder

It began in Minya, Upper Egypt.

ZLYM grew out of the personal experience of its founder, Rawan Adel Abdulmalik, pursuing scientific research opportunities from Minya. She found that motivation is not the same as access to guidance, resources, mentors and a supportive environment, and that this access is not equally distributed.

Instead of stopping, she began helping students around her find research pathways, learning resources and competitions, and develop their own ideas. That led to a bigger question: what if young people in different communities and countries could help one another build what their communities are missing?

The movement is meant to become a structure that other young people shape, lead and carry forward. It is still in its founding stage, and its network and international presence are being developed.

Rawan Adel Abdulmalik · Minya, Egypt

FAQ

Questions, answered honestly.

Is ZLYM a scholarship or opportunity announcement page?

No. Its central purpose is to help young people identify local needs, develop projects, and learn through action and collaboration. Information may be shared when useful, but announcements are not the movement's main work.

Do I need to have a project idea before joining?

No. Curiosity, commitment, and willingness to contribute are a good starting point. Members can learn how to understand a need and shape an idea with support.

Does ZLYM already operate in every country?

Not yet. ZLYM is in its founding stage. Country representation will be announced only after ambassadors are confirmed.

Is participation only online?

The network connects members online, while local projects may take place in communities. Any in-person activity must be planned responsibly and follow appropriate safeguarding and guardian-consent requirements.

Can I join if I have no previous experience?

Yes. Previous experience or a completed project is not required for general membership.

Can I apply to become a Country Ambassador?

Yes. Interested applicants can submit the Country Ambassador application form. Applying is an expression of interest, not an automatic appointment.

How can an organization collaborate with ZLYM?

Organizations can contact the movement to introduce themselves, explain the community they serve, and describe the kind of collaboration they have in mind.
